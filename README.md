# Introduction-to-Computers

It contains a full journey through eight decades of machines that learned to think, from a timeline of the core technology behind each era. Comparing the Generations, speed, size and technology.

## Introduction to Computers
A computer is an electronic device that manipulates data. Every generation of computing is defined by a shift in core circuit technology:
* **Speed:** From thousands to trillions of operations per second.
* **Size:** From room-sized machines to microchips.
* **Intelligence:** From fixed instructions to adaptive systems.

## Types of Computers
* **Supercomputers:** High-performance systems for complex calculations.
* **Mainframes:** Large-scale enterprise and institutional systems.
* **Microcomputers:** Desktops, laptops, and modern mobile devices.

## History of Computers: Five Generations
1. **1st Generation (1940–1956) - Vacuum Tubes:** ENIAC and room-sized heavy machines (~10,000 ops/sec).
2. **2nd Generation (1956–1963) - Transistors:** Refrigerator-sized systems (~1 million ops/sec).
3. **3rd Generation (1964–1971) - Integrated Circuits:** Cabinet-sized systems (~10 million ops/sec).
4. **4th Generation (1971–2010) - Microprocessors:** Desktop and laptop era (billions of ops/sec).
5. **5th Generation (2010–Present) - AI & Machine Learning:** Modern pocket-to-cloud systems.
